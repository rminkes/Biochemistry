**Concept.** Lysosomal storage diseases result from deficiency of a specific lysosomal enzyme, so
its substrate accumulates inside lysosomes and causes progressive cellular and organ dysfunction.
Two groups are relevant here: **sphingolipidoses** (sphingolipid accumulation — Tay-Sachs,
Niemann-Pick, Gaucher, Krabbe, Fabry, metachromatic leukodystrophy) and **mucopolysaccharidoses**
(glycosaminoglycan accumulation — Hurler, Hunter). Most are autosomal recessive; the **X-linked**
exceptions are **Fabry and Hunter**. Many present in infancy or childhood with neurodegeneration,
hepatosplenomegaly, and/or skeletal and facial changes. Recognition turns on three things: the
deficient enzyme, the accumulated substrate, and the discriminating clinical clue (cherry-red
macula, hepatosplenomegaly, corneal clouding, coarse facies).

**Key facts.**
- A lysosomal enzyme deficiency → substrate accumulates in lysosomes → cellular dysfunction.
- Sphingolipidoses (sphingolipids) vs mucopolysaccharidoses (glycosaminoglycans).
- Most are autosomal recessive; X-linked exceptions: Fabry and Hunter.
- Discriminating clues: cherry-red macula, hepatosplenomegaly, corneal clouding, coarse facies.

**Clinical correlation.** Pattern recognition by enzyme/substrate/clue; enzyme replacement therapy
exists for some (Gaucher, Fabry).

**Differentiating traps.**
- X-linked = Fabry and Hunter; the rest are autosomal recessive.
- Cherry-red macula = Tay-Sachs and Niemann-Pick (Niemann-Pick adds hepatosplenomegaly; Tay-Sachs
  has none).
- Hurler (corneal clouding) vs Hunter (no corneal clouding, aggressive behavior).

**Vignette signature.** Enzyme/substrate/clinical clue → specific disease (see summary table).

**Mnemonic.** "X-linked lysosomal storage = Fabry and Hunter; everything else autosomal recessive."

**Concept.** Tay-Sachs disease is autosomal recessive deficiency of **hexosaminidase A**, causing
accumulation of **GM2 ganglioside**, especially in neurons. It presents in infancy with progressive
neurodegeneration (developmental regression, loss of milestones), an exaggerated startle reflex
(hyperacusis), and a **cherry-red spot on the macula**, but **no hepatosplenomegaly** — which
distinguishes it from Niemann-Pick. Lysosomes show "onion-skin" whorled membranes. It is more common
in the Ashkenazi Jewish population and is fatal in early childhood.

**Key facts.**
- Hexosaminidase A deficiency; AR; ↑GM2 ganglioside.
- Progressive neurodegeneration, exaggerated startle (hyperacusis), cherry-red macula.
- No hepatosplenomegaly (vs Niemann-Pick).
- "Onion-skin" lysosomes; Ashkenazi Jewish predominance; fatal early.

**Clinical correlation.** An infant with regression, exaggerated startle, a cherry-red spot, and
*no* organomegaly.

**Differentiating traps.**
- Tay-Sachs: cherry-red macula + *no* hepatosplenomegaly (vs Niemann-Pick, which has it).
- Hexosaminidase A; GM2 ganglioside.

**Vignette signature.** "Infant with developmental regression, exaggerated startle, cherry-red
macula, and no hepatosplenomegaly" → Tay-Sachs.

**Mnemonic.** "Tay-Sa**X** = hexosaminidase **A**; cherry-red, no spleen."

- Sphingomyelinase; foam cells.

**Vignette signature.** "Infant with neurodegeneration, hepatosplenomegaly, and cherry-red macula" →

### I-cell disease

```yaml
title: I-cell disease (mucolipidosis II)
category: lysosomal storage
system: structural
enzyme: N-acetylglucosaminyl-1-phosphotransferase
inheritance: autosomal recessive
high_yield: true
tags: [i-cell, mucolipidosis-II, mannose-6-phosphate, enzyme-targeting, high-plasma-enzymes, gingival-hyperplasia]
cases: []
flashcards:
  - q: "Which enzyme is deficient in I-cell disease (mucolipidosis II)?"
    a: "N-acetylglucosaminyl-1-phosphotransferase."
  - q: "Which targeting tag fails to be added to lysosomal enzymes in I-cell disease?"
    a: "Mannose-6-phosphate (M6P)."
  - q: "In I-cell disease, where do the lysosomal enzymes end up instead of the lysosome?"
    a: "Secreted into the plasma (blood) — plasma lysosomal enzyme levels are high."
  - q: "Conceptually, what kind of defect is I-cell disease?"
    a: "A defect in lysosomal enzyme trafficking/targeting — not a single degradative-enzyme deficiency."
  - q: "Which oral finding is characteristic of I-cell disease?"
    a: "Gingival hyperplasia."
  - q: "Concept → diagnosis: coarse facies, gingival hyperplasia, corneal clouding, with HIGH plasma lysosomal enzyme levels."
    a: "I-cell disease (mucolipidosis II)."
references:
  - "LIR §Lysosomal enzyme targeting"   # primary (biochemistry)
```

**Concept.** I-cell disease (mucolipidosis II) is autosomal recessive deficiency of
**N-acetylglucosaminyl-1-phosphotransferase**, the enzyme that adds the **mannose-6-phosphate (M6P)**
tag to lysosomal enzymes. Without the M6P targeting signal, lysosomal enzymes are not delivered to
lysosomes but are instead **secreted into the blood** — so lysosomes lack their enzymes and
accumulate substrate (forming inclusion bodies, the "I-cells"), while **plasma lysosomal enzyme
levels are high**. It presents with coarse facial features, **gingival hyperplasia**, clouded
corneas, restricted joint movement, and skeletal abnormalities, and is fatal in childhood. The key
mechanism point: this is a defect in enzyme *trafficking* (M6P), not in a single degradative enzyme.

**Key facts.**
- N-acetylglucosaminyl-1-phosphotransferase deficiency; AR.
- Fails to add the mannose-6-phosphate (M6P) tag → lysosomal enzymes are secreted, not delivered.
- Lysosomes lack enzymes (inclusion bodies, "I-cells"); plasma enzyme levels are *high*.
- Coarse facies, gingival hyperplasia, corneal clouding, joint restriction; fatal in childhood.

**Clinical correlation.** A severe coarse-facies/skeletal phenotype, with elevated plasma lysosomal
enzymes as the diagnostic clue.

**Differentiating traps.**
- I-cell = M6P targeting defect (trafficking), not a single degradative enzyme.
- High *plasma* lysosomal enzymes (enzymes are mis-secreted).
- Links to the mannose-6-phosphate protein-targeting concept.

**Vignette signature.** "Coarse facies, gingival hyperplasia, and corneal clouding, with high plasma
lysosomal enzyme levels" → I-cell disease.

**Concept.** This module consolidates the lysosomal storage diseases by enzyme, accumulated
substrate, inheritance, and discriminating clue; each is detailed above. The two recurring
high-yield discriminators: the **cherry-red macula pair** (Tay-Sachs without organomegaly vs
Niemann-Pick with hepatosplenomegaly) and the **mucopolysaccharidosis pair** (Hurler with corneal
clouding, AR; Hunter without, X-linked, aggressive).

**Key facts.**
- Cherry-red macula: Tay-Sachs (no organomegaly) and Niemann-Pick (hepatosplenomegaly).
- X-linked: Fabry and Hunter; all others autosomal recessive.
- Characteristic cells: Gaucher (crumpled tissue paper), Niemann-Pick (foam), Krabbe (globoid).
- I-cell = mannose-6-phosphate targeting defect; high plasma lysosomal enzymes.

**Clinical correlation.** Match the discriminating clue (cherry-red macula, characteristic cell,
corneal clouding, angiokeratomas) to the enzyme.

**Differentiating traps.**
- Cherry-red pair distinguished by organomegaly; mucopolysaccharidosis pair by corneal clouding +
  inheritance.
- I-cell is a trafficking defect, not a degradative-enzyme defect.

**Vignette signature.** Clue → disease (see table).

## Section answer key

**Lysosomal storage overview**
1. Sphingolipidoses (sphingolipid accumulation) and mucopolysaccharidoses (glycosaminoglycan
   accumulation).
2. Fabry and Hunter.

**Tay-Sachs disease**
1. Hexosaminidase A.
2. The absence of hepatosplenomegaly (Niemann-Pick has it; both share the cherry-red macula).

**Niemann-Pick disease**
1. Sphingomyelinase.
2. The presence of hepatosplenomegaly (Tay-Sachs lacks it).

**Gaucher disease**
1. Glucocerebrosidase (β-glucosidase).
2. A lipid-laden macrophage with "crumpled tissue paper" cytoplasm.

**Krabbe disease**
1. Galactocerebrosidase.
2. Globoid cells (multinucleated macrophages).

**Fabry disease**
1. α-galactosidase A.
2. X-linked recessive.

**Metachromatic leukodystrophy**
1. Arylsulfatase A.
2. Sulfatides (cerebroside sulfate).

**Hurler syndrome**
1. α-L-iduronidase.
2. Corneal clouding.

**Hunter syndrome**
1. Iduronate-2-sulfatase.
2. No — Hunter syndrome does *not* involve corneal clouding.

**I-cell disease**
1. Mannose-6-phosphate (M6P).
2. Secreted into the blood (plasma lysosomal enzyme levels are high).

**Lysosomal storage disease summary**
1. Tay-Sachs and Niemann-Pick; Tay-Sachs has no hepatosplenomegaly, whereas Niemann-Pick does.
2. I-cell disease (mucolipidosis II).
