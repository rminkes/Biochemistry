**Concept.** The TCA cycle is the mitochondrial hub of aerobic metabolism, oxidizing acetyl-CoA
(derived from carbohydrate, fat, and protein) to CO₂ while capturing energy in reduced carriers.
Acetyl-CoA condenses with oxaloacetate (**citrate synthase**) to form citrate; over one turn each
acetyl-CoA yields **3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, and 1 GTP**, releases 2 CO₂, and regenerates oxaloacetate.
The GTP is made by *substrate-level phosphorylation* at the succinyl-CoA → succinate step
(succinyl-CoA synthetase). The rate-limiting enzyme is **isocitrate dehydrogenase** (activated by
ADP, inhibited by ATP and NADH). The NADH and FADH₂ feed the electron transport chain for
oxidative phosphorylation. The cycle is **amphibolic** — its intermediates also supply
biosynthesis (oxaloacetate and α-ketoglutarate for amino acids, succinyl-CoA for heme) — so they
must be replenished (anaplerosis, e.g., by pyruvate carboxylase). Per glucose (2 acetyl-CoA) the
cycle yields 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂, and 2 GTP.

**Key facts.**
- Mitochondrial; oxidizes acetyl-CoA → 2 CO₂, regenerating oxaloacetate.
- Per acetyl-CoA: 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP (substrate-level), 2 CO₂.
- Rate-limiting: isocitrate dehydrogenase (+ ADP; − ATP/NADH).
- GTP made at succinyl-CoA → succinate (substrate-level phosphorylation).
- α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase uses the same 5 cofactors as PDH (TPP, lipoic acid, CoA, FAD, NAD⁺).
- Amphibolic; intermediates feed biosynthesis; replenished by anaplerosis.

**Clinical correlation.** α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, like PDH, is thiamine-dependent and
arsenic-sensitive. Succinyl-CoA is a heme precursor (links to heme synthesis).

**Differentiating traps.**
- The cycle's only direct high-energy phosphate is GTP (substrate-level), not ETC-derived ATP.
- Isocitrate dehydrogenase = rate-limiting.
- α-KG dehydrogenase shares cofactors and regulation with PDH.

**Vignette signature.** "Step generating GTP by substrate-level phosphorylation" → succinyl-CoA →
succinate. "Rate-limiting TCA enzyme" → isocitrate dehydrogenase.

**Mnemonic.** Intermediates: "**C**itrate **I**s **K**rebs' **S**tarting **S**ubstrate **F**or
**M**aking **O**xaloacetate" — Citrate, Isocitrate, α-Ketoglutarate, Succinyl-CoA, Succinate,
Fumarate, Malate, Oxaloacetate.

### Electron transport chain & oxidative phosphorylation

```yaml

    a: "NADH at Complex I, FADH₂ at Complex II (so FADH₂ yields slightly less ATP)."
  - q: "Which complexes pump protons?"

    a: "Molecular O₂, reduced to water at Complex IV (cytochrome c oxidase)."
  - q: "What does ATP synthase (Complex V) do?"
    a: "Lets protons flow back into the matrix, using that energy to phosphorylate ADP to ATP (chemiosmosis)."
  - q: "What does it mean that electron transport and ATP synthesis are coupled?"
    a: "ATP is made only as protons return through ATP synthase."
  - q: "Trap: is O₂ a proton pump?"
    a: "No — O₂ is the final electron acceptor, not a proton pump."
references:
  - "LIR §Electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation"   # primary (biochemistry)
```

**Concept.** The electron transport chain (ETC), in the inner mitochondrial membrane, is where
most ATP is generated. NADH and FADH₂ donate electrons that pass through a series of complexes:
NADH enters at **Complex I**, FADH₂ at **Complex II** (so FADH₂ yields slightly less ATP). As
electrons flow toward the final acceptor — molecular **O₂**, reduced to water at Complex IV
(cytochrome c oxidase) — Complexes I, III, and IV pump protons from the matrix into the
intermembrane space, building an electrochemical gradient (the proton-motive force). **ATP
synthase (Complex V)** then lets protons flow back into the matrix, harnessing that energy to
phosphorylate ADP to ATP — the chemiosmotic coupling Mitchell described. Electron transport and
ATP synthesis are therefore normally **coupled**: ATP is made only as protons return through ATP
synthase. Without O₂ as the terminal acceptor, the chain backs up and oxidative phosphorylation
halts.

**Key facts.**
- Inner mitochondrial membrane; NADH → Complex I, FADH₂ → Complex II.
- Complexes I, III, IV pump protons → proton-motive force; O₂ is the terminal acceptor (→ water at IV).
- ATP synthase (Complex V) makes ATP as protons flow back (chemiosmosis).
- Electron flow and ATP synthesis are coupled.
- Most of a glucose's ATP comes from oxidative phosphorylation.

**Clinical correlation.** This chain is the target of the inhibitors and uncouplers in the next
module; mitochondrial myopathies impair specific ETC complexes.

**Differentiating traps.**
- NADH enters at Complex I (more ATP); FADH₂ at Complex II (less).
- Proton pumping (I, III, IV) builds the gradient; ATP synthase (V) uses it.
- O₂ is the final electron acceptor, not a proton pump.

**Vignette signature.** "Final electron acceptor of the chain" → O₂. "Enzyme using the proton
gradient to make ATP" → ATP synthase (Complex V).

**Mnemonic.** "Electrons fall down the chain to O₂; the protons pumped out return through ATP
synthase."

### ETC inhibitors & uncouplers

```yaml

    a: "Rotenone = Complex I; antimycin A = Complex III; cyanide/CO/azide = Complex IV."
  - q: "What does oligomycin inhibit, and what happens?"
    a: "ATP synthase (Complex V); the proton gradient builds and backs up electron transport."
  - q: "Name the uncouplers of oxidative phosphorylation."
    a: "2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP), high-dose salicylates, and thermogenin (UCP1) in brown fat."
  - q: "How do uncouplers affect O₂ consumption and ATP?"
    a: "They increase O₂ consumption but decrease ATP, dissipating the gradient as heat (hyperthermia)."
  - q: "Key discriminator: inhibitors vs uncouplers on O₂ use?"
    a: "Inhibitors decrease O₂ use and ATP (lactic acidosis); uncouplers increase O₂ use but decrease ATP (heat)."
  - q: "Cyanide vs CO — which complex, and treatments?"
    a: "Both hit Complex IV; treat cyanide with hydroxocobalamin/nitrites/thiosulfate, CO with 100% O₂."
  - q: "Vignette: increased O₂ consumption with decreased ATP and hyperthermia — agent type?"
    a: "An uncoupler (2,4-DNP or high-dose salicylates)."
references:
  - "LIR §Inhibitors of oxidative phosphorylation"   # primary (biochemistry)
  - "KATZUNG §Cyanide and CO poisoning"               # cross-reference (pharmacology/tox)
```

**Concept.** Agents disrupt oxidative phosphorylation in two distinct ways. **Electron transport
inhibitors** block flow at a specific complex, stopping proton pumping and therefore ATP
synthesis: **rotenone** (Complex I), **antimycin A** (Complex III), and **cyanide / carbon
monoxide / azide** (Complex IV). These halt the chain, so O₂ can't be used and cells switch to
anaerobic metabolism (lactic acidosis). **ATP synthase inhibitors** such as **oligomycin** block
Complex V directly; the proton gradient builds and, unable to dissipate, slows electron transport
by back-pressure. **Uncouplers** — **2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP)**, **high-dose salicylates**, and the
physiologic **thermogenin (UCP1)** in brown fat — make the inner membrane leaky to protons,
dissipating the gradient as heat; electron transport then runs *fast* (O₂ consumption rises) but
ATP synthesis *falls*, with energy lost as heat (hyperthermia). Clinical anchors: cyanide poisoning
(fire smoke, nitroprusside) treated with hydroxocobalamin/nitrites/thiosulfate; CO poisoning
(cherry-red, treat with 100% O₂); and 2,4-DNP causing dangerous hyperthermia.

**Key facts.**
- Electron transport inhibitors (stop the chain → no ATP, lactic acidosis): rotenone (I),
  antimycin A (III), cyanide/CO/azide (IV).
- ATP synthase inhibitor: oligomycin (V) → gradient builds, electron transport backs up.
- Uncouplers (leaky membrane, gradient → heat; O₂ use ↑, ATP ↓): 2,4-DNP, high-dose salicylates,
  thermogenin (UCP1, brown fat).
- Cyanide/CO → Complex IV; treat cyanide (hydroxocobalamin, nitrites, thiosulfate), CO (100% O₂).

**Clinical correlation.** Cyanide (fire smoke, nitroprusside) and CO poisoning are board staples;
2,4-DNP (an illicit weight-loss agent) causes fatal hyperthermia; neonatal brown-fat thermogenesis
uses physiologic uncoupling.

**Differentiating traps.**
- Inhibitors *decrease* O₂ use and ATP (with lactic acidosis); uncouplers *increase* O₂ use but
  *decrease* ATP (heat). This O₂-consumption direction is the key discriminator.
- Oligomycin blocks ATP synthase, secondarily slowing electron transport (coupling back-pressure).
- Cyanide and CO both hit Complex IV.

**Vignette signature.** "Increased oxygen consumption but decreased ATP, with hyperthermia" →
uncoupler (2,4-DNP / high-dose salicylates). "Cherry-red skin in a fire victim" → CO. "Bitter-
almond odor, or after nitroprusside" → cyanide.

**Mnemonic.** Sites: "Rotenone-**1**, Antimycin-**3**, Cyanide/CO-**4**, Oligomycin-**5**; DNP
uncouples."
