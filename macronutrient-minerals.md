### Protein-calorie malnutrition: marasmus vs kwashiorkor

```yaml
title: Protein-calorie malnutrition (marasmus vs kwashiorkor)
category: macronutrient
system: nutrition
high_yield: true
tags: [marasmus, kwashiorkor, protein-energy-malnutrition, edema, fatty-liver, hypoalbuminemia]
cases: []
flashcards:
  - q: "Marasmus — primary deficiency and key features?"
    a: "Total calorie (energy) deficiency → severe muscle and fat wasting, emaciation, and no edema."
  - q: "Kwashiorkor — primary deficiency and key features?"
    a: "Protein deficiency (calories relatively adequate) → edema, fatty liver, distended belly, skin/hair changes, apathy."
  - q: "Why does kwashiorkor cause edema?"
    a: "Hypoalbuminemia lowers oncotic pressure."
  - q: "Trap: which has edema and a fatty liver — marasmus or kwashiorkor?"
    a: "Kwashiorkor (protein); marasmus is wasting without edema (calories)."
  - q: "Vignette: child with edema, distended belly, fatty liver, and skin/hair changes — diagnosis?"
    a: "Kwashiorkor."
references:
  - "LIR §Protein-energy malnutrition"        # primary (biochemistry)
  - "ROBBINS §Marasmus and kwashiorkor"        # cross-reference (pathology)
```

**Concept.** Protein-energy malnutrition takes two classic forms. **Marasmus** is a total
**calorie (energy) deficiency**: severe muscle wasting and loss of subcutaneous fat, emaciation, and
a "skin and bones" appearance in a child who is often alert but severely underweight — and notably
**no edema**. **Kwashiorkor** is primarily a **protein deficiency** (with relatively adequate
calories): the hallmark is **edema** (from low oncotic pressure due to hypoalbuminemia), along with
a swollen/distended belly and **fatty liver** (hepatomegaly), skin and hair changes (depigmentation,
the "flag sign"), and an apathetic child.

**Key facts.**
- Marasmus = total calorie deficiency → muscle/fat wasting, emaciation, *no* edema.
- Kwashiorkor = protein deficiency (calories relatively adequate) → edema (hypoalbuminemia), fatty
  liver, distended belly, skin/hair changes, apathy.

**Clinical correlation.** Distinguish by edema and fatty liver (kwashiorkor) versus pure wasting
without edema (marasmus).

**Differentiating traps.**
- Edema + fatty liver = kwashiorkor (protein); wasting without edema = marasmus (calories).
- Kwashiorkor edema is from low albumin / reduced oncotic pressure.

**Vignette signature.** "Child with edema, distended belly, fatty liver, and skin/hair changes" →
kwashiorkor. "Emaciated child with severe wasting and no edema" → marasmus.

**Mnemonic.** "**K**washiorkor: protein **K**onked out → edema/fatty liver. **M**arasmus: **M**uscle
wasting (energy), no edema."

**Marasmus vs kwashiorkor**

| Feature | Marasmus | Kwashiorkor |
|---|---|---|
| Primary deficiency | Total calories (energy) | Protein (calories adequate) |
| Edema | Absent | Present (hypoalbuminemia) |
| Liver | Not fatty | Fatty liver / hepatomegaly |
| Body habitus | Severe wasting, emaciation | Edematous, distended belly |
| Other | Often alert | Apathy, skin/hair changes |

1. Which form of protein-calorie malnutrition presents with edema and a fatty liver? *(→ key)*
2. What is the main deficiency in marasmus? *(→ key)*

### Trace minerals (zinc, copper, selenium, iodine)

```yaml
title: Trace minerals (zinc, copper, selenium, iodine)
category: minerals
system: nutrition
high_yield: true
tags: [zinc, copper, selenium, iodine, wound-healing, lysyl-oxidase, keshan, goiter]
cases: []
flashcards:
  - q: "Zinc deficiency features?"
    a: "Poor wound healing, hypogeusia (impaired taste), alopecia, perioral/acral dermatitis, impaired immunity, hypogonadism."
  - q: "What is the inherited zinc-malabsorption disorder?"
    a: "Acrodermatitis enteropathica."
  - q: "Copper's roles, and its deficiency features?"
    a: "Cofactor for lysyl oxidase, cytochrome c oxidase, ceruloplasmin; deficiency → anemia, neutropenia, connective-tissue fragility."
  - q: "Trap: Menkes vs Wilson copper status?"
    a: "Menkes = genetic copper deficiency; Wilson = copper overload."
  - q: "Selenium deficiency causes what?"
    a: "Cardiomyopathy (Keshan disease) — selenium is part of glutathione peroxidase."
  - q: "Iodine deficiency causes what?"
    a: "Goiter, hypothyroidism, and (in pregnancy) congenital hypothyroidism/cretinism — iodine is needed for T3/T4 synthesis."
references:
  - "LIR §Trace minerals"        # primary (biochemistry)
  - "ROBBINS §Trace element deficiencies"   # cross-reference (pathology)
```

**Concept.** Four trace minerals are high-yield. **Zinc** is a cofactor for many enzymes (important
for wound healing and the senses of taste and smell); deficiency causes poor wound healing,
hypogeusia (impaired taste), alopecia, perioral/acral dermatitis (the inherited zinc-malabsorption
form is **acrodermatitis enteropathica**), impaired immunity, and hypogonadism. **Copper** is a
cofactor for lysyl oxidase (collagen/elastin cross-linking — see Menkes in the connective-tissue
section), cytochrome c oxidase, ceruloplasmin (iron metabolism), and others; deficiency causes
anemia (impaired iron use), neutropenia, connective-tissue/vascular fragility, and neurologic
problems (Menkes is genetic copper *deficiency*; Wilson disease is copper *overload*). **Selenium**
is a component of glutathione peroxidase (an antioxidant); deficiency causes a cardiomyopathy
(**Keshan disease**). **Iodine** is required for thyroid hormone (T3/T4) synthesis; deficiency causes
goiter, hypothyroidism, and — in pregnancy — congenital hypothyroidism (cretinism).

**Key facts.**
- Zinc: enzyme cofactor (wound healing, taste/smell); deficiency → poor wound healing, hypogeusia,
  alopecia, dermatitis (acrodermatitis enteropathica), hypogonadism.
- Copper: lysyl oxidase, cytochrome c oxidase, ceruloplasmin; deficiency → anemia, neutropenia,
  connective-tissue fragility (Menkes = genetic; Wilson = overload).
- Selenium: glutathione peroxidase (antioxidant); deficiency → cardiomyopathy (Keshan disease).
- Iodine: thyroid hormone (T3/T4) synthesis; deficiency → goiter, hypothyroidism, cretinism.

**Clinical correlation.** Zinc — poor wound healing and loss of taste; iodine — goiter; selenium —
cardiomyopathy.

**Differentiating traps.**
- Zinc deficiency → poor wound healing, loss of taste, acrodermatitis enteropathica.
- Copper → lysyl oxidase (connective tissue) and ceruloplasmin (links to Menkes/Wilson).
- Selenium → Keshan cardiomyopathy; iodine → goiter/cretinism.

**Vignette signature.** "Poor wound healing, loss of taste, perioral/acral dermatitis" → zinc.
"Goiter and hypothyroidism" → iodine. "Cardiomyopathy from a dietary deficiency" → selenium (Keshan).

**Mnemonic.** "Zinc = wound healing + taste; Copper = cross-linking (lysyl oxidase); Selenium =
cardiomyopathy (Keshan); Iodine = thyroid."

**Check.**
1. Deficiency of which trace mineral causes poor wound healing and loss of taste? *(→ key)*
2. Which trace mineral is required for thyroid hormone synthesis? *(→ key)*

### Starvation & metabolic fuel timeline

```yaml
title: Starvation & metabolic fuel timeline
category: macronutrient
system: nutrition
high_yield: true
tags: [starvation, fasting, glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis, ketogenesis, protein-sparing]
cases: []
flashcards:
  - q: "Primary source of blood glucose in the first ~24 hours of fasting?"
    a: "Hepatic glycogenolysis (lasts roughly 12–24 hours)."
  - q: "After glycogen is depleted, what maintains blood glucose?"
    a: "Gluconeogenesis (from lactate, glycerol, and glucogenic amino acids)."
  - q: "What ramps up as fasting extends into days?"
    a: "Fatty acid oxidation and ketogenesis; the brain shifts toward ketone bodies."
  - q: "In prolonged starvation, what spares protein, and what limits survival?"
    a: "Ketones spare muscle protein; fat stores ultimately limit survival."
  - q: "What is the order of fuel use in fasting?"
    a: "Glycogenolysis → gluconeogenesis → ketogenesis."
references:
  - "LIR §Integration of metabolism"          # primary (biochemistry)
  - "GUYTON §Starvation and metabolic fuels"    # cross-reference (physiology)
```

**Concept.** This module ties the fed/fasting transitions from Metabolism — Foundations into a
timeline. In the **fed state** (insulin high), glucose is stored as glycogen and fat is synthesized.
In **early fasting** (hours), blood glucose is maintained first by **hepatic glycogenolysis** (which
lasts roughly 12–24 hours), then by **gluconeogenesis** (from lactate, glycerol, and glucogenic
amino acids). As fasting extends into days, **fatty acid oxidation and ketogenesis** ramp up, and the
brain shifts from glucose toward **ketone bodies**, sparing protein. In **prolonged starvation**
(weeks), maximal ketone use by the brain minimizes glucose need and protein breakdown, so muscle
protein is preserved as long as possible, and survival is ultimately limited by fat stores. Glucagon,
epinephrine, and cortisol drive the fasting program.

**Key facts.**
- Fed (insulin): glycogen and fat synthesis.
- Early fasting: hepatic glycogenolysis (~12–24 h) → gluconeogenesis (lactate, glycerol, amino acids).
- Days: fatty acid oxidation + ketogenesis rise; brain shifts to ketones, sparing protein/glucose.
- Prolonged starvation (weeks): ketones spare muscle protein; fat stores limit survival.
- Hormones: glucagon, epinephrine, cortisol drive fasting.

**Clinical correlation.** Explains hypoglycemia once glycogen depletes, ketosis in starvation, and
protein-sparing adaptation.

**Differentiating traps.**
- Order: glycogenolysis (first ~1 day) → gluconeogenesis → ketogenesis.
- Ketones spare protein in prolonged starvation (the brain adapts to use them).
- Glycogen stores last roughly a day.

**Vignette signature.** "Fuel source maintaining glucose in the first day of a fast" → hepatic
glycogenolysis. "Why protein is spared in prolonged starvation" → the brain uses ketones.

**Mnemonic.** "Fasting order: **G**lycogen → **G**luconeogenesis → **K**etones (then fat runs out)."

**Check.**
1. What is the primary source of blood glucose during the first ~24 hours of fasting? *(→ key)*
2. In prolonged starvation, what fuel does the brain rely on to spare protein? *(→ key)*
